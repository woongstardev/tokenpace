Tokens per second measures how quickly a language model produces its answer. On its own, though, the number is hard to interpret. A token is neither a character nor a word — it is a fragment the model settled on during training.
In English, one token usually maps to a short word or a piece of one. At forty tokens per second, roughly two hundred characters scroll past every second. That is already faster than most people can follow.
Korean behaves differently. With the same tokenizer, one or two Hangul syllables typically collapse into a single token. Forty tokens per second becomes something closer to seventy characters per second on screen. That gap is why a benchmark number can look impressive and still feel slow in practice.
So how fast is fast enough? The reference point is not the benchmark, it is human reading speed. Past that threshold, raising the number changes almost nothing you can feel. What starts to matter instead is how long you wait before the first character appears.
